# 01. Vision

## The game in one paragraph

AI personas arrive in a small world with nothing. They farm, gather, craft,
trade and talk, one in-game hour at a time. The world's goal changes from one
run to the next. The player does not steer a persona during a run: the
player **writes** one (its character, its priorities, what it remembers and
how), sends it into the world, watches the replay, and rewrites it. The game
is the craft of the persona.

## For whom

- **Proposed**: people curious about agents who want to experiment without
  writing code: a persona is a file, not a program.
- **Proposed**: a second audience, people who build agents and want a fair
  ground to compare designs (memory strategies, prompts, models).

## What makes it different

- **Living together is scored.** Most agent arenas reward individual
  performance; here the world's survival, honest trades and reputation
  count (see 07-scoring.md). A predatory persona can win a run and lose the
  game.
- **What is carried over is what we watch.** A persona keeps a memory across
  runs; the interesting persona is the one that learns what to keep.
- **Every action is a governed tool call.** The world can run under mesh7
  policies, which makes governance part of the play, not a lecture.
- **Replays explain.** A run is a log anyone can replay and read: why a
  persona starved, who lied in a trade.

## Not the game (proposed)

- Not a real-time game: one turn is one in-game hour.
- Not a combat game.
- Not a chat with characters: the personas talk to each other, the player
  reads.

## Open

- The name's meaning (hoshi, star in Japanese) and whether the world has a
  setting beyond "a small world": see the art direction in 08-visuals.md.
